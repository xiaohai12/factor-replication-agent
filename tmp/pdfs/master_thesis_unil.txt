                                    Abstract


Factor replication is often treated as binary, although discrepancies can reflect paper
interpretation, data environment, portfolio construction, or estimand. This thesis de-
velops a controlled LLM-agent workflow for making those alternatives auditable. The
agent proposes an evidence-backed MethodSpec from a paper, subject to human re-
view, and generates only the signal-computation code after approval. All consequen-
tial empirical settings are selected from a fixed, reviewable menu. The resulting signal
is frozen before any configuration experiment, so all empirical results are deterministic
conditional on the approved specification and data snapshot.

The workflow is applied to three illustrative equity anomalies. We compare the papers’
reported endpoints with C&Z’s independent human replication and run the frozen
agent signal under standardized HXZ configurations. AssetGrowth is reproduced on
the aligned raw-return metric, but its magnitude is materially sensitive to configura-
tion choices. MeanRankRevGrowth exhibits a residual-dominated paper comparison,
showing that a gap cannot automatically be assigned to the agent when important con-
ventions are unresolved or outside the menu. For ShareVol, the paper reports a Fama–
MacBeth coefficient whereas the available replications are portfolio-sort spreads; the
system therefore declines a paper-anchored numerical decomposition rather than com-
paring incommensurable estimands.

Where factorial designs are feasible, Shapley values provide exact within-engine attri-
bution of configuration effects; larger designs receive OAT sensitivity analysis with its
interaction limitation explicit. External comparisons and three-term identities remain
observational accounting devices. The contribution is thus not an autonomous empir-
ical judge, but an agent-assisted, human-reviewed process that turns paper interpreta-
tions into versioned research objects and makes remaining replication gaps traceable
rather than opaque.

1    INTRODUCTION

Consider the sales-growth reversal signal in Lakonishok et al. (1994) – what Chen and
Zimmermann code as MeanRankRevGrowth. Hou et al. (2020) report that under their
standardized replication protocol – value-weighted portfolios, NYSE breakpoints – the
signal fails to clear the conventional significance bar (t = 1.08). Chen and Zimmermann
(2022), implementing the same paper’s own stated method rather than a uniform ex-
ternal protocol, replicate it with a t-statistic of 3.94, and classify it as a clear predictor.
Same factor, same underlying economic idea, the same published text to work from –
and opposite verdicts, from two teams that are each careful, well-resourced, and work-
ing independently.

This is not an isolated case, and it does not require picking a side. Across several
hundred overlapping anomalies, Hou et al. (2020) report that 65.4% fail to clear |t| ≥
1.96 under their uniform protocol, while Chen and Zimmermann (2022) report that
all but a handful of the same literature’s characteristics replicate under each paper’s
own stated method. These two numbers are not answers to the same question – one
is a failure rate under an externally imposed protocol, the other is a failure rate under
paper-specific implementation choices – so it would be a mistake to read them as a
simple contradiction. But that very fact is itself the point: the question "does this factor
replicate?" does not have a single, protocol-free answer, because a published paper
does not, in general, uniquely determine every choice its own replication requires.

The right question is therefore not whether a factor replicates, but why two careful,
independent teams can reach opposite verdicts on the same factor – and whether that
disagreement can be attributed to specific, falsifiable causes (a breakpoint convention,
a weighting rule, a lag assumption) rather than treated as an unexplained clash of
protocols. Answering it requires two things: a controlled execution engine that enu-
merates the menu of implementation choices a paper admits, so that gaps between
two implementations can be decomposed field by field rather than reported as one
opaque number; and an independent extraction of what the paper itself actually says,
uncontaminated by prior exposure to either team’s implementation, so that a given
disagreement can be attributed to a specific side’s departure from the text rather than
merely to "different choices." This second requirement is where a controlled LLM agent

enters – not as a labor-saving substitute for a human replicator, but as an auditable,
independently-arrived-at reading of the paper – every field it extracts traceable to a
cited quote, and arrived at without ever seeing either team’s implementation – that can
serve as a neutral referee between two existing, human-produced implementations.

Applying this framework to three factors chosen specifically because Hou-Xue-Zhang’s
and Chen-Zimmermann’s own reported verdicts on them differ in kind rather than
merely in degree (§5): sales-growth reversal, this section’s opening example, whose
HXZ-versus-C&Z conflict is attributed to a compounding breakpoint-and-weighting
effect rather than to either choice alone; asset growth (Cooper et al., 2008), the one
case in this sample where both teams agree the signal is significant; and share turnover
(Datar et al., 1998), whose original test is a firm-level Fama-MacBeth regression rather
than a portfolio sort at all, so that HXZ’s and C&Z’s quantile-sort implementations are
both equally-unauthorized reconstructions of the paper’s actual method, not compet-
ing readings of a shared design. Running our controlled pipeline on these factors, we
find that even in the one case where the published verdicts agree throughout (asset
growth), the largest term in the agent’s own three-way gap decomposition against the
C&Z reference spread is not the configuration difference between implementations but
the agent’s own replication residual, which accounts for 67% of the total gap – more
than the configuration and signal/environment terms combined. Related evidence
holds in both disagreement cases, though of a different kind in each. For sales-growth
reversal, the agent’s replication residual alone exceeds the total observed gap in mag-
nitude (partially offset by an opposing configuration term) – a residual effect layered
on top of the breakpoint-and-weighting story already given above for its configuration
side. For share turnover, the agent’s own signal is not robust across the four alternative
breakpoint/weighting configurations tested against it (a t-statistic range of 0.79 and
one outright sign flip), indicating that at least part of the disagreement under study
is intrinsic to the signal rather than attributable to either team’s protocol. Together,
both cases show that the agent’s own replication precision, not just protocol choice, is
a first-order determinant of the measured gap. Part of that replication residual, in turn,
is itself a scope limitation rather than a reading error: this engine’s fixed configuration
menu cannot always express an off-menu instruction the paper actually gives – the
same standardization tradeoff HXZ’s own protocol makes in this paper’s opening ex-
ample – a distinction §7 and §8 draw out factor by factor. The remainder of this section
states two research questions this design answers (§1.1), fixes the unit of evaluation

1.1     Two research questions

RQ1 (attribution). For a given factor, how much of the total gap between an exter-
nal reference (CZ or HXZ) and the paper’s own reported result (P) is attributable to
a difference in signal construction (and environment), how much to a difference in
portfolio- construction configuration, and how much to the agent’s own inability to
replicate the paper exactly? §3’s paper-anchored three-term identity answers this by
construction rather than by inference: each of the three terms is an exact difference be-
tween two tracks this paper’s controlled engine actually runs, not a residual assigned
by assumption, and the identity holds for every factor whose own headline result is a
portfolio-sort spread, regardless of whether CZ and HXZ agree or conflict. Where the
paper’s own headline is instead a regression coefficient (share turnover, §8), P is not
a quantity the identity’s terms can be taken against at all, and that definitional gap is
reported directly rather than forced into the three terms.

RQ2 (capability boundary). Producing the paper-faithful configuration that RQ1’s
baseline, A, depends on is itself a nontrivial task for the agent system (§1.2): what
fraction of high-impact MethodSpec fields does it resolve without a human correction,
what fraction of runs reach an executable A end-to-end, and where does human correc-
tion concentrate – on purely technical mismatches, or on fields where the paper leaves
a genuine empirical judgment unstated? This is reported as an independent finding
(C2), not merely as a caveat on RQ1: it costs nothing beyond what RQ1’s own pipeline
already produces, since every MethodSpec field the agent resolves (or fails to) while
building A is logged regardless of whether it is ever used for RQ1’s decomposition
(§7).



1.2     Measurement unit convention

The unit under evaluation throughout this paper is not a bare language model call;
it is the agent system as a whole – the LLM, the tool interface it uses to query paper
text and prior evidence, the Step 2 review gate, the Step 4 sandbox validator, and the


Shengzhao Lei                                         Underdetermination in Factor Replication
                                                                                   11 (58)


human confirmation step that resolves any field the reviewer flags (§4). Human con-
firmation is a designed component of this system, not an external intervention that
spoils the measurement: a real deployment of this pipeline always includes a human
confirming ambiguous fields before A is built, so excluding that step from the unit of
evaluation would understate, not idealize, what the deployed system actually does.
Consequently, RQ2’s autonomy footprint (§1.1) is measured as where the agent’s own
designed checkpoints trigger – which fields reach the reviewer, which of those the re-
viewer resolves without escalating to a human, and which require a human correction
– rather than as an estimate of raw LLM reading accuracy against some hypothetical
unsupervised baseline. This distinction matters for how the capability results in §7
should be read: a high rate of human correction on a given field is evidence that this
system’s checkpoints are doing their job, not, by itself, evidence that the underlying
model reads the paper poorly.



1.3   Contributions

   • C1 (RQ1, empirical, main thread). For each of three factors, decompose the total
      gap between CZ (or HXZ) and P into a signal/environment term, a configuration
      term, and an agent-residual term via three controlled counterfactual tracks (A,
      Acz , Ahxz ) – an exact identity.

   • C2 (RQ2, agent capability, independent finding). Report the agent’s autonomy
     footprint and capability boundary across the same three factors, as a free byprod-
      uct of running RQ1 – no separate annotation corpus required.

   • C3 (methodological foundation for both). A controlled, auditable, LLM-assisted-
     but-not-LLM-controlled replication pipeline – every extracted field carries a cited
      quote and source location, the extraction step is leakage-proof by construction
      (§3), and every empirical number is deterministically reproducible with the LLM
      switched off.

2     RELATED LITERATURE

2.1   Factor replication: HXZ, C&Z, and JKP

This paper joins a decade-long methodological debate about how to replicate the pub-
lished cross-sectional return-predictability literature. Hou et al. (2020) rerun 452 anoma-
lies under one uniform protocol – NYSE breakpoints, value weighting – and report that
65.4% fail to clear |t| ≥ 1.96; the failure rate falls to 43.1% under an equal-weighted
protocol with NYSE-Amex-NASDAQ breakpoints, a fact HXZ themselves use to ar-
gue that weighting and breakpoint conventions, not the underlying economics, drive
much of the disagreement. Their contribution is an aggregate failure rate under a sin-
gle, externally imposed protocol; it does not decompose any individual factor’s gap
into a signal component and a configuration component, nor does it compare against
the paper’s own originally reported number field by field.

Chen and Zimmermann (2022) take the opposite approach: for each of 319 characteris-
tics, they implement the paper’s own stated method as closely as possible, and report
that all but a handful replicate. Where their result disagrees with HXZ’s, they make
a manual, case-by-case judgment of whether the anomaly itself is invalid or whether
the standardized protocol departs from what the paper actually specified. This is a
substantive contribution, but it stops short of an actual counterfactual: C&Z never run
their own signal through HXZ’s protocol, or vice versa, to isolate how much of the
disagreement is attributable to the signal formula versus the portfolio configuration.

Jensen et al. (2023) instead replace the entire protocol with a uniform Bayesian frame-
work and report a sequence of six failure rates (35% → 55.6% → 61.3% → 82.4% →
75.6% → 82.4%) as successive methodological choices are changed one at a time –
comparable tercile/decile conventions, exclusion of originally-insignificant anomalies,
CAPM-alpha rather than raw returns, and multiple-testing corrections. This is the clos-
est existing work to a decomposition, but it decomposes the effect of replacing the pro-
tocol, not the disagreement between two independently-arrived-at implementations of
the same protocol space.

2.2   Researcher degrees of freedom and multiverse analysis

The idea that a single dataset admits many defensible analytical choices, and that the
choice matters, is well established outside finance. Specification-curve analysis (Si-
monsohn et al., 2020) and multiverse analysis (Steegen et al., 2016) both enumerate
the full space of reasonable choices a researcher could make and report how the result
varies across that space. The finance literature’s own version of this concern is the
p-hacking / multiple-testing critique of anomaly discovery.

This paper’s disagreement band is narrower by construction, and that is a deliberate
design choice rather than a limitation to apologize for: rather than enumerate every
choice a researcher could imaginably make, it is calibrated to the choices two real, in-
dependent, well-resourced implementers (the agent and Chen-Zimmermann) actually
disagreed on. The resulting band is an empirically observed lower bound on the pa-
per’s true implementation space (§3), not an a priori enumeration of hypothetical alter-
natives – it is anchored to what real disagreement looks like, not to what disagreement
could in principle look like.



2.3   LLM agents for research automation

A growing literature uses large language models to automate parts of the empirical re-
search pipeline, from literature extraction to code generation, and a parallel literature
raises validity concerns about doing so – principally that an LLM’s output can silently
encode choices the model was never authorized, or expected, to make. This paper’s
design responds to that concern directly rather than by assertion: the LLM is only
ever permitted to author the compute_signal function (§4); it never sets an empirical
parameter – weighting, breakpoint convention, sample window, or estimator – all of
which are drawn from a fixed, human-auditable configuration menu. Every quantity
reported in §7 is therefore reproducible with the LLM switched off, once a plugin has
been frozen; the LLM’s role is confined to producing an artifact whose downstream
consequences are then measured by a deterministic engine, not to producing the em-
pirical findings themselves.

3     CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK

This section formalizes the notation used throughout the paper and states two identi-
ties the empirical design in §6 is built to measure. It deliberately contains no imple-
mentation detail – those are deferred to §4 and Appendix B.



3.1   Notation

For a given factor and its published paper P, six quantities recur: P, the spread (and
t-statistic) P itself reports; A, the result of running the agent’s independently-extracted
signal S A under the agent’s own reading of P’s configuration, Cagent – the paper-faithful
baseline; Acz and Ahxz , hybrid tracks that hold S A fixed but swap in Chen and Zimmer-
mann’s actual configuration, Ccz , or an HXZ-style standardized configuration, Cstd , re-
spectively; and CZ and HXZ, each team’s own independently measured result for the
same factor (Chen and Zimmermann’s own reported number, and Hou-Xue-Zhang’s
own reported number where one exists). A track is one (signal, configuration) pair exe-
cuted by the engine; a freeze commits a signal or configuration to evidence so that later
tracks can be compared against it without risk of silent drift (§4).

Definition 1 (Underdetermination). Given a paper P, let I( P) denote the set of imple-
mentations it admits. If {verdict(i ) : i ∈ I( P)} is not a singleton, the factor’s replication
verdict is underdetermined by P.

Definition 2 (Disagreement band). The subspace spanned by the difference set be-
tween A and CZ is used as an empirical lower-bound estimate of I( P) (a lower bound
because the true disagreement space can only be wider).


The disagreement band operationalizes underdetermination: I( P) itself cannot be
enumerated, since doing so would require running every implementation the paper’s
text admits, so the band substitutes what can actually be measured – the gap between
two real, independently-arrived-at points inside that space – for the full space itself.

The HXZ side substitutes Ahxz for Acz symmetrically. Each term has a distinct, non-
interchangeable meaning: the first isolates how much of the gap survives even when
the configuration is held at C&Z’s own choices – attributable to differences between
the agent’s signal and C&Z’s signal, plus any residual data-vintage or engine effects
that Q2 (§4, future work) would need to separate further; the second isolates the con-
figuration effect alone, with the signal held fixed at S A ; the third is the agent’s own
departure from the paper, holding neither signal nor configuration against an external
party. Collapsing these into one number – "the total gap" – is exactly what existing
replication studies do (§2), and exactly what this identity is designed not to do.

The second term is clean specifically because Acz (symmetrically, Ahxz ) and A are
both computed by this engine, from the same signal S A , rather than one side being
C&Z’s – or HXZ’s – own separately-coded output. Comparing CZ directly to HXZ, by
contrast, would confound whatever configuration difference separates the two teams
with whatever infrastructure differences separate their two independently built code-
bases (data vintage, rounding, engine-level conventions); the three-term identity exists
specifically so that this confound never enters the configuration term, at the cost of
relegating it to the (unidentified, see §4) CZ − Acz term instead. This does not make
the engine’s own finite configuration menu (§4, Appendix B) free of cost – §5.2 states
that cost directly – only that whatever the menu’s limits are, they are applied identi-
cally to A, Acz , and Ahxz , so the configuration term measures a configuration effect and
nothing else.

This identity only licenses the "who deviates from the paper" reading on fields P ex-
plicitly states; on fields P is silent on, there is no ground truth to deviate from, and the
analysis reverts to the disagreement-band framing above. Both readings coexist in this
paper’s results (§7): a stated field is scored as an attributable deviation, a silent field is
scored as a point in an underdetermined space. They are not alternative framings of
the same evidence – they are the correct framing for two disjoint kinds of configuration
field.

3.3   The LLM-assisted, not LLM-controlled, principle

Everything in this framework depends on one constraint holding without exception:
the language model is permitted to author the compute_signal function and nothing
else. Every quantity that enters Cagent – weighting rule, breakpoint convention, sample
window, holding period, estimator – is drawn from a fixed menu enforced mechani-
cally by registry.build_config (§4, Appendix B); a value the paper appears to specify
but that falls outside the menu is clamped to a documented default rather than passed
through as the model’s own choice. This is what makes A usable as a paper-faithful
baseline rather than merely another model output: the model’s influence is confined
to one auditable artifact, and every downstream number in §7 is reproducible by re-
running the deterministic engine against a frozen compute_signal with the language
model switched off entirely.

The same discipline is what makes Cagent auditable rather than merely inspectable af-
ter the fact: every field the Step 1 extractor emits carries a cited quote and source
location (§4), so a reviewer can check the model’s reading against the paper’s own
text field by field instead of accepting its output on faith. One specific guarantee this
auditability depends on is a leakage-proof boundary: Chen and Zimmermann’s own
metadata (SignalDoc.csv) is used only for post-hoc evaluation – to compute CZ and
to select which factors exhibit informative HXZ-versus-C&Z divergence (§5) – and is
never passed to the extraction step that produces Cagent . Section 1 already flags where
this boundary is a system-level guarantee and where it instead depends on the disci-
pline of a human reviewer; that distinction is restated in full in §8.

4     THE CONTROLLED REPLICATION PIPELINE

Figure 4.1 summarizes the eight-step agent pipeline that produces every quantity in
§7. The language model has a strictly limited role: it may extract a method from paper
text (Step 1), iteratively review and rewrite that extraction against the paper text before
a human sees it (Step 2), write the signal formula (Step 3), and, only if requested, draft
an explanation of already-computed evidence (Step 8). Each step below states what its
prompt is given and constrained to return; the prompt text itself, and the code that as-

than reproduced here.1 All empirical choices and every reported number are instead

4.1   Step 1: extract a MethodSpec from the paper

Step 1 extracts a structured MethodSpec from the paper’s text (and, when available,
the paper’s raw PDF), together with a live registry of the data sources and columns
this pipeline supports – so a required field’s source_table/source_column is resolved
against what is actually registered rather than invented. The resulting specification
covers paper identity, the signal definition, these data-catalog bindings, the sample
periods, timing, universe, and portfolio-construction fields, and, not least, the paper’s
own reported headline results (its metrics, t-statistics, and risk-adjustment models),
which later steps benchmark against. Wherever a field has direct textual support, it
carries an evidence citation – a verbatim quoted span for text, or a table/row/column
reference for a value read off a table – rather than every field carrying its own free-
standing provenance.

Step 1’s draft is not the last time the paper is read: Step 2’s review loop (below) is
explicitly permitted to consult the paper text again, and every change it makes is
captured as a diff against Step 1’s draft. No step beyond Step 2 ever reads the pa-
per directly, so every later step’s notion of "what the paper says" is mediated through
the final, human-approved MethodSpec and its evidence trail, rather than through an
unaudited, free-form re-reading of the paper.



4.2   Step 2: LLM-assisted review, then a human resolution gate

Step 2 opens with an LLM-assisted review loop, before any human sees the extracted
MethodSpec. Each round validates the current specification, runs deterministic tool
checks (schema validation and engine-menu/capability findings), and sends the pa-
per text, the current specification, and those tool results to the LLM, which returns a
rewritten specification together with structured notes on any value corrections it made.
The loop repeats for up to a fixed number of rounds or until a round leaves the specifi-
cation unchanged; every field-level change the LLM makes is captured as a mechanical
before/after diff, so nothing it rewrites is applied silently. A small set of identifier fields
(factor_id, schema_version, the paper’s document_id) is never taken from the LLM
and is re-injected deterministically every round.




Shengzhao Lei                                           Underdetermination in Factor Replication
                                                                                     21 (58)


Only once this loop converges does the human review gate take over: a human con-
firms or corrects any remaining low-confidence or inconsistent fields, each intervention
is retained as field-level evidence, and the gate verifies source registration and blocks
an unknown signal-input source.

A universe filter whose concept must be derived – e.g. mapping the paper’s stated
categories onto an underlying column’s raw encoding, or computing a concept from
other fields – is reviewed together with that derivation rather than applied or dropped
silently; if it cannot yet be resolved against any registered data source, a human must
explicitly mark it accepted_unapplied with a reason, rather than the stated restriction
being silently ignored, and the converse decision is recorded just as explicitly: a filter
inferred from indirect evidence can instead be marked human_confirmed_applied (the
two are mutually exclusive).

Only a specification with direct textual support or an explicit human resolution for ev-
ery required field is marked codegen_ready. This gate also produces the RQ2 extrac-
tion measure (§1): the fraction of high-impact fields resolved without assistance is read
from this ordinary review record, not a separate annotation exercise. "High-impact" is
a fixed, enumerated set of fields whose value materially changes the backtest – the sig-
nal’s direction; the formation rule, rebalance frequency, and holding period; the portfo-
lio’s construction type, weighting, and return-combination rule; the universe descrip-
tion; and, per sort dimension or required data field, the breakpoint basis, grouping,
and sort mode, the missing-data policy, and the source-table/column binding – rather
than a per-field judgment made at review time; only fields in this set are scored, and
every one of them counts as high-impact.



4.3   Step 3: generate the signal and freeze the configuration

Once a MethodSpec is codegen-ready, its generator prompt is given only that approved
specification and returns only a compute_signal function body; the pipeline assembles
this into one complete standalone backtest script. The prompt has no access to, and
cannot generate, lags, universe, breakpoint, weighting, or portfolio-construction logic.
registry.build_config then turns the approved MethodSpec into an executable con-
figuration by resolving every relevant field against a fixed menu of allowed values
(weighting rule, breakpoint convention, rebalance frequency, holding period, universe

Shengzhao Lei                                        Underdetermination in Factor Replication
                                                                                     22 (58)


filters, estimator; the full menu is reproduced in Appendix B). A value the Method-
Spec specifies that falls outside this menu is not passed through – it is deterministically
clamped to a documented default, and the clamp is logged in config["defaults_applied"]
rather than silently dropped. This is the mechanism that makes I( P) (§3) something
one can actually enumerate: because the menu is finite and explicit, every configura-
tion this paper ever runs is a labeled point in a known space, not an unbounded piece
of hand-written logic.

This control is also an operational limitation of the present agent, rather than a claim
that every paper can be reproduced without extension. A factor is admissible only
when its required inputs are available through the registered data sources and links,
and its empirical design can be expressed in the engine menu. For example, the cur-
rently implemented weighting alternatives are equal weighting (EW) and value weight-
ing (VW); a paper requiring another weighting rule is recorded as an out-of-menu de-
viation rather than approximated by generated code. The study-level data and design
boundaries— US common equity, monthly CRSP returns, and the supported Compu-
stat/IBES signal inputs and link tables—are stated fully in §5.2.



4.4   Step 4: validate the generated artifact in a sandbox

Step 4 validates the generated compute_signal plugin in a sandbox, before it is allowed
to run against real data, through a fixed sequence of checks: syntax, schema, a static
scan for forbidden look-ahead patterns (e.g. shifting a series into the future), and, last,
an execution smoke test. That final check is not purely static: it imports the assembled
backtest script and actually runs compute_signal against a small, fixed slice of secu-
rities’ full monthly history, checking for raised exceptions, the returned schema and
dtype, missing-value rates, and degenerate portfolio groupings (e.g. an empty long or
short leg) that only surface once the signal is actually computed.

Any of these checks can trigger the same bounded repair loop. On a failure, an auto-
mated repairer is given only the validator’s raw error report and rewrites the compute_signal
plugin itself – never the surrounding, deterministically assembled script, and never an
empirical parameter; the full script is then reassembled from this repaired plugin and
validated again, for up to a fixed number of attempts before the batch is returned to
a human. The loop does not distinguish where the failure came from – a look-ahead

Shengzhao Lei                                        Underdetermination in Factor Replication
                                                                                     23 (58)


finding, a degenerate portfolio grouping, and a runtime exception all trigger a plu-
gin repair the same way a syntax error does – so the accurate constraint is that only
technical or execution failures are repairable at this stage, never an empirical formula
choice.



4.5   Step 5: execute the frozen backtest script

Step 5 runs the standalone script assembled in Step 3 in a fresh subprocess against the
actual resolved data, as a single one-track run – this is not the resource-limited smoke
test of Step 4, but the real execution that produces the paper’s replicated backtest.
The standardized engine supplies the approved universe, lags, breakpoints, weight-
ing, portfolio construction (including any winsorization or other transform Step 2’s
review configured, applied by the engine rather than by the plugin), and return cal-
culation; the plugin supplies only the signal formula. Execution produces the run’s
track-level metrics: mean monthly return, a Newey-West t-statistic, Sharpe ratio, and
month count, together with CAPM/FF3/FF5 alphas and their own t-statistics when
factor data is available. The same mean/t-stat/Sharpe are additionally recomputed
separately over three windows – the paper’s own in-sample period, the gap between
the sample’s end and the paper’s publication, and the post-publication period – so that
any decay in the signal after the sample the paper studied, or after its publication, is
visible rather than averaged away.

The execution boundary is deliberately split between two components. BacktestRunner
builds the standalone script from the reviewed MethodSpec, the frozen compute_signal
source, and a resolved configuration, then launches that exact file in a fresh subprocess.
Inside the script, BacktestExecutor implements the fixed ten-step lifecycle: data load-
ing, delisting-return treatment, missing-data policy, universe filtering, excess-return
conversion, signal holding-period expansion, portfolio formation (including break-
points), portfolio-return calculation, long–short construction, and metric calculation.
Thus, the generated artifact contributes the signal formula only; it cannot replace or
silently modify the standardized backtesting lifecycle. This separation is what makes
a Step 6 comparison a configuration experiment over one frozen signal rather than a
comparison of separately generated backtest programs.

4.6   Step 6: compare tracks and implementation variants

Step 6 orchestrates several tracks built from the same frozen signal code: the original-
specification track (no overrides), a standardized track built against a fixed HXZ con-
figuration, an optional track built from a human-supplied C&Z configuration, and a
set of attribution tracks that each isolate one or more configuration switches – break-
point source (NYSE-only vs. all-exchange), weighting rule (EW vs. VW), accounting
lag, rebalance frequency (always changed together with holding period, so the num-
ber of observations does not silently change), universe filters, and breakpoint quantiles.
When two tracks being compared differ on a small number of these switches, the con-
troller runs the full factorial of baseline/target combinations across them; beyond that
it falls back to flipping one switch at a time, so a batch’s exact number of attribution
runs depends on how many switches the comparison at hand actually touches, not a
fixed count. Every track is built from the exact same plugin object, varying only its
configuration overrides. The review UI overlays every track’s cumulative long-short
return on one chart per sample-period segment (in-sample, the gap between sample
end and publication, and post-publication, mirroring the segmentation in §4’s Step 5
metrics), so a divergence between, say, A and Ahxz is visible directly rather than only
as a difference in summary statistics.



4.7   Step 7: decompose the replication gap and preserve evidence

Step 7 builds two decompositions from a factor’s executed tracks. A field-level de-
composition attributes the gap between Acz (or Ahxz ) and A to individual configura-
tion fields – exactly, via the full factorial, when few enough fields differ, and via the
one-at-a-time design otherwise (Equation (6.1), §6). A second, paper-anchored decom-
position applies Equation (3.1) itself, splitting the gap between an external reference
(CZ or HXZ) and P into the same three named terms defined in §3, together with a
check that they sum to the total gap. Every comparison the bundle reports also carries
the identification level §6 assigns it, so a reader can tell a controlled attribution from a
merely descriptive one.

The resulting evidence bundle is considerably larger than these two decompositions: it

also carries the field-level configuration diff, a specification-quality summary, publication-
decay and robustness sections, and an external-performance comparison, among oth-
ers, all indexed under a single flat evidence_keys whitelist – which is what the next
step is restricted to citing.



4.8   Step 8: optionally draft an evidence-constrained diagnosis

Step 8 is the one place in the pipeline where the LLM is asked to write about results
rather than produce them. Its input is exactly the evidence bundle Step 7 already com-
puted, plus the paper-evidence quotes already recorded in the reviewed MethodSpec.
Its output is drawn from a small, fixed menu of claim types, each a natural-language
rendering of one already-computed comparison: whether the paper’s and the agent’s
sign agree; how large a magnitude or significance gap between two tracks is; how
much of a gap a given configuration switch, or the joint set of switches, explains;
whether the factor’s own return pattern decays after the paper’s publication; and, for
the paper-anchored three-term identity, which of the three terms dominates an exter-
nal party’s distance from the paper. Every claim must cite the specific evidence behind
it and is tagged as a proposal rather than a finding. The LLM does not compute, see
the derivation of, or alter any number, and it does not decide the overall verdict – it
only selects which of these fixed, pre-defined things is true for this factor and phrases
it in prose. This is what lets the pipeline surface a human-readable explanation for a
factor’s replication gap without reopening, at the one point the model is allowed to
speak about the results, any guarantee the rest of the pipeline establishes.

5     DATA AND FACTOR SAMPLE

5.1   Data sources

Portfolio returns are constructed from the CRSP Monthly Stock File, including delist-
ing returns; accounting inputs come from Compustat Fundamentals Annual, linked to
CRSP through the CCM link table with a point-in-time accounting lag (accounting_lag_months).
IBES’s unadjusted summary statistics (annual EPS forecast consensus) are likewise reg-
istered, linked to CRSP via a dedicated IBES–CRSP link table, though none of the three
factors in §5.3 draws on it. Every source is declared once in a single catalog (§4) rather
than accreted ad hoc: each entry states how that source’s own native key links, through
a point-in-time link table, to the CRSP permno backbone every source is ultimately
merged onto. Adding a further source is a single declarative entry in this catalog, after
which any paper drawing on it is handled the same way as CRSP/Compustat, rather
than requiring new source-specific code; conversely, a paper whose required source
has no such entry is hard-blocked at Step 2’s review rather than silently improvised.
This is also what makes every field, join key, and lag behind a number in §7 traceable
to one place.

Chen and Zimmermann’s factor metadata (SignalDoc.csv) plays a strictly circum-
scribed role: it supplies CZ (each factor’s reported spread and t-statistic under C&Z’s
own implementation) and the evidence used to select factors in §5.3, and nothing
else – it is never passed to the Step 1 extractor that produces Cagent (§3). The HXZ-
style standardized configuration Cstd used to construct Ahxz is a single, versioned file
(data/hxz/hxz_standard_config.yaml), applied identically across every factor rather
than tuned per factor.



5.2   Scope and assumptions

This paper’s implementation menu (Appendix B) is restricted along several dimen-
sions that are worth stating positively rather than apologetically: restricting attention
to US common equity, monthly portfolio returns, and CRSP–Compustat accounting

6.3   Field-level gap decomposition

Our objective is to allocate the difference between the baseline Cagent and a target con-
figuration (Ccz or Cstd ) to the individual configuration fields that change. Let D be the
set of those fields, and let R(S) denote the in-sample mean return from the track that
switches exactly the fields in S ⊆ D to their target values, leaving all other fields at
their baseline values. In particular, R(∅) is the baseline result: no field is switched and
every field retains its Cagent value.

For example, suppose only the weighting rule w and breakpoint rule b differ. The full
factorial grid runs four tracks: R(∅), R({w}), R({b}), and R({w, b}). The contribu-
tion of w is the average of its effect when switched first, R({w}) − R(∅), and when
switched after b, R({w, b}) − R({b}); b is treated symmetrically. Thus, any effect that
arises only when both fields are switched is shared rather than assigned arbitrarily to
whichever field is considered first.

allocate the total difference by Shapley values:

the change in absolute t-statistics, rather than applying undefined logarithms.



6.5   Identification levels and significance tiers

Identification levels are evidence labels: they do not change any estimated return, t-
statistic, or Shapley value, but determine the strength of the interpretation we permit.
They are derived automatically from the experiment design and available evidence,
never hand-labeled. Controlled evidence is a complete factorial grid, whose Shapley at-
tribution exactly allocates the total mean-return difference across the switched fields; it
supports high-strength attribution claims. At the individual-track level, a comparison
is also labeled controlled when exactly one configuration or snapshot axis differs. Har-
monized evidence is an OAT or otherwise single-switch comparison with the remain-
ing settings held fixed; it supports medium-strength claims about that comparison, but


Shengzhao Lei                                          Underdetermination in Factor Replication
                                                                                     33 (58)


does not identify interactions or guarantee additivity. Observational evidence is a dif-
ference that is observed without an isolating experiment, including external published
references and raw multi-configuration differences; it supports only low-strength de-
scriptive claims. Unidentified means the evidence required for an attribution was not
produced – for example, a multi-field endpoint contrast without the requisite facto-
rial or OAT runs – and likewise supports only low-strength limitation claims. This
hierarchy prevents an observed replication gap from being reported as a controlled
configuration effect when the experiment did not isolate it. Significance is reported at
three tiers – |t| ≥ 1.96, 2.78, and 3.39 – matching the thresholds Hou et al. (2020) them-
selves use under increasingly conservative multiple-testing corrections, rather than a
single conventional cutoff.

7.5   Evidence-constrained LLM diagnosis

Step 8 was run as an optional explanation layer for all three completed comparison
bundles. It produced 25 persisted diagnosis claims (ten for AssetGrowth, nine for
MeanRankRevGrowth, and six for ShareVol), with no claim rejected by the evidence
validator. Each accepted claim names one or more keys from the deterministic bundle
and inherits its identification level and evidence strength. For example, the Asset-

In these cases, this provides an illustrative, bounded analytical capability: the model

8     DISCUSSION, LIMITATIONS AND CONCLUSION

8.1   Implications

The results show why a single comparison between two published factor implementa-
tions is an inadequate diagnosis. A difference can arise from a controlled configuration
change, from the signal and data environment that remain outside the experiment,
from a mismatch between the engine and the paper’s requested method, or from an
estimand that is not comparable in the first place. AssetGrowth demonstrates that
substantial configuration effects can coexist with agreement about a factor’s sign and
significance. MeanRankRevGrowth demonstrates that a large paper-comparison resid-
ual cannot automatically be attributed to the agent when the implementation menu
supplies defaults for unresolved or unsupported fields. ShareVol demonstrates the
stronger boundary: a paper coefficient and a portfolio-sort spread should not be forced
into the same replication identity.

The agent’s contribution is not to replace the researcher’s empirical judgment, but to
turn an unstructured paper description into a conversational, reviewable, and exe-
cutable research object. It extracts a MethodSpec with source evidence and explicit
uncertainty labels, and generates only the signal-computation code after that specifi-
cation is approved; consequential empirical settings remain governed by human re-
view and a fixed menu. Freezing the resulting agent signal then permits the same pa-
per interpretation to be run under multiple configurations, separating implementation
sensitivity from variation that would arise if the model rewrote the strategy each time.
A researcher can of course reproduce an individual factor manually. The incremental
value of the agent workflow is to extend that exercise across papers while retaining a
consistent evidence standard, review interface, and versioned audit trail.

The controlled factorial results are therefore the paper’s strongest evidence. They iso-
late the effect of moving the same frozen signal between configurations and allocate
that within-engine difference exactly by Shapley values. OAT results remain useful
for sensitivity analysis, but their interaction caveat prevents an additive causal read-
ing. External comparisons and the three-term identity are valuable accounting devices,

yet remain observational because they absorb external code, data-vintage, sample-

Robustness in this study does not mean that a factor is invariant across all plausible im-
plementations. Rather, the controlled results show different patterns: AssetGrowth re-
mains positive and significant across the baseline, C&Z, and standardized-HXZ config-
urations, while its return magnitude and t-statistic change materially; MeanRankRevGrowth
weakens under the standardized-HXZ configuration; and ShareVol’s portfolio-sort tracks
remain insignificant across the configurations examined. The separate-window results
add a temporal qualification: a successful paper-window replication need not persist
after publication. AssetGrowth’s baseline loses significance in its post-publication win-
dow, whereas MeanRankRevGrowth remains significant in its own post-publication
window. Robustness is thus reported as auditable evidence conditional on the cho-
sen configurations and windows, not as proof of a factor’s general validity; the post-
publication comparisons are descriptive and do not identify a causal effect of publica-
tion.



8.2     Limitations

8.2.1    Agent and specification limits

   • No within-agent dispersion study. Repeated independent extractions of each
        paper have not been run. Consequently, the analysis cannot separate variation
        induced by model sampling from ambiguity in the paper or from a genuine ex-
        traction error.

   • No cross-model or prompt-sensitivity evaluation. The study does not compare
        the selected agent model, system prompt, or tool-use policy with alternative
        models or prompting strategies. Its findings therefore describe this controlled
        workflow, not a model-agnostic ranking of LLMs or a claim that the same extrac-
        tion quality will transfer to every agent configuration.

   • No blinded evaluation of the diagnosis layer. Step 8’s proposal is structurally
     constrained by deterministic evidence keys and identification labels, but this
        study does not yet compare its claim selection or explanatory usefulness with
        blinded expert judgments.

Shengzhao Lei                                          Underdetermination in Factor Replication
                                                                                      47 (58)


   • Finite configuration and estimator menu. The engine clamps off-menu or un-
        specified choices to documented defaults. This preserves reproducibility, but
        means a paper-faithful baseline can contain a scope deviation. It also excludes
        methods such as Fama–MacBeth from the controlled portfolio-sort experiment.

   • No external-signal bridge. C&Z’s independently coded signal has not been ex-
     ecuted through this engine. The term labelled signal-and-environment therefore
        combines signal, data-vintage, and engine differences rather than identifying a
        pure signal effect.


8.2.2    Experimental and statistical limits

   • Factorial budget. Complete factorial attribution is limited to three differing fields.
        Larger contrasts use OAT and cannot identify interactions; ShareVol is the present
        example.

   • External comparability. The paper, C&Z, HXZ, and engine endpoints may use
        different windows, data vintages, standard-error conventions, or estimands. Ex-
        act arithmetic closure of the three-term identity does not remove these differ-
        ences.

   • t-channel domain. The log-t decomposition is available only when both com-
        pared mean returns are positive and the required signs are consistent. Otherwise
        the system reports an absolute-t change but does not manufacture a channel split.

   • Small, illustrative sample. Three deliberately selected factors cannot estimate
     how frequently each diagnosis occurs in the factor literature.



8.3     Future work

The immediate next step is repeated extraction per paper, which would turn the cur-
rent single-draw audit into a measured within-agent dispersion study. A bridge that
runs an external implementation through the same engine and data snapshot would
further separate signal differences from environmental ones. Extending the menu to
additional data sources, estimator families, and portfolio constructions would reduce
selection and clamping limits. Finally, scaling the protocol to a pre-specified broader

factor sample would test whether the three observed diagnostic patterns generalize
beyond these illustrative cases.



8.4   Conclusion

This paper treats replication not as a binary verdict, but as a question of which differ-
ences can be measured, compared, and identified. It therefore separates controlled con-
figuration contrasts from observational external accounting and from cases in which
the relevant estimands are not comparable at all.

The agent’s role is deliberately bounded. It turns an unstructured paper into an evidence-
backed MethodSpec with explicit uncertainty, generates only the approved signal-
computation code, and then has that signal frozen for every subsequent configuration
experiment. Human review and the fixed configuration menu continue to govern con-
sequential empirical choices. This makes the agent’s paper interpretation a versioned,
auditable object rather than a black box that can silently alter the strategy while an
experiment is running.

Across the three cases, AssetGrowth is a raw-return reproduction that remains mate-
rially configuration-sensitive; MeanRankRevGrowth has a residual-dominated paper
comparison; and ShareVol’s paper coefficient cannot be identified with a portfolio-sort
spread. Where complete factorial grids are feasible, the workflow reports exact within-
engine Shapley contributions; where they are not, it reports non-additive OAT sensitiv-
ity; and where estimands differ, it declines the paper-anchored decomposition. These
boundaries are part of the result: they make the remaining replication gap auditable
rather than presenting it as an opaque failure of either the factor or the agent.

A     METHODSPEC SCHEMA

The MethodSpec produced by Step 1 and reviewed in Step 2 (§4) is implemented as
roughly 35 nested Pydantic models in src/infra/models/method_spec.py, up to five
levels deep. Reproducing the full definition here would not aid legibility; this ap-
pendix instead gives the two recurring building blocks that carry provenance through
the spec, the nine top-level sections of MethodSpec itself, and one representative deep
branch (the portfolio-construction tree). The complete, authoritative schema is the
source file itself.1



A.1     Two recurring building blocks

Most leaf fields in MethodSpec are not plain values: they are wrapped so that a value
can never appear without a record of whether, and how, it is supported by the paper.

    • EvidenceCitation: location, quote (a verbatim span of paper text), table_ref
      (table, row, column, used instead of quote when the value comes from a table
       rather than prose), interpretation.
    • SourcedValue[T]: value (of type T), evidence (a list of EvidenceCitation), status
       (one of the EvidenceStatus values – e.g. directly supported, table-only, human-
       resolved, or unsupported), and, when unsupported, an unsupported_value place-
       holder rather than a silently invented one.

Not every field is SourcedValue-wrapped or carries its own evidence list – a few struc-
tural or purely definitional fields (e.g. an enum selecting among a fixed menu of con-
struction types) are plain values – but every field that encodes an empirical choice read
from the paper is.

A.2    Top-level structure

   • paper (PaperRef): document id, title, citation, publication year.
   • signal (SignalSpec): the signal’s definition, economic intuition, and direction
      (all SourcedValue), a signal-category enum, and a FormulaSpec decomposing
      the paper’s formula into discrete CalculationSteps with their own inputs, con-
      stants, and evidence.
   • data (DataSpec): signal/return frequency, the sources cited, and a list of RequiredFields
      – each one a concept referenced in the paper together with the source_table and
     source_column it resolves to against the registered data catalog (§4).
   • sample (SampleSpec): three independently tracked periods – data coverage, portfolio-
      formation, and reported-returns – each a Period with its own evidence.
   • timing (TimingSpec): formation rule and month, rebalance frequency, holding
      period, and a DataAvailability record of the reporting lag applied before a sig-
      nal can be used.
   • universe (UniverseSpec): a description plus a list of FilterSpec inclusion/exclusion
     rules, each tied to a concept and an evidence-backed operator/value.
   • portfolio (PortfolioSpec): construction type, sort dimensions, portfolio legs,
      weighting scheme, return-combination rule, missing-data policies, and signal
      transforms – detailed below.
   • reported_results (ReportedResults): the paper’s own headline numbers – up
     to four ReportedMetrics (estimand, adjustment model, weighting, estimate, statis-
      tic, sample period, each with evidence) plus an optional ComparisonDerivation
      recording how a high-minus-low spread is built from two of them. This is the sec-
      tion the gap decomposition in §7 benchmarks against; it is not incidental meta-
      data.
   • notes: free-text, non-evidentiary.

D     VALIDATOR AND LEAKAGE-CONTROL RULES

Step 4 validates the exact standalone script assembled after code generation, but its
repair scope is deliberately technical: it can reject or repair a syntax, schema, or exe-
cution defect, never an empirical choice that belongs to human review. The generated
plugin contains only compute_signal; all timing, universe, breakpoints, and portfolio
construction remain in controlled engine components.

    • Information boundary. The extraction step is never given SignalDoc.csv; that
      file is evaluation-only. Data availability lags are applied in the data layer before
      the plugin receives its input, and plugins are forbidden from implementing their
      own lag policy.
    • Static checks. The validator parses the plugin, requires the declared entry func-
      tion, scans for known future-looking constructs (including negative shifts, .future,
      and lead(), and enforces any factor-specific market-equity implementation pol-
      icy.
    • Execution check. The exact generated script is imported in a separate process
      and its signal function is executed on a small, point-in-time real-data slice. The
      output must have the required keys and a numeric signal; a raised exception,
      timeout, non-numeric output, or clearly invalid signal cardinality/leg coverage
      fails validation.
    • Diagnostics rather than hidden repair. Empty or degenerate thin-slice output is
      recorded as a warning when it may reflect coverage. The full Step 5 execution
      remains the final check of the complete engine on the full data. Only technical
      errors are eligible for bounded repair.
    • Optional faithfulness read. When enabled, a separate LLM checks whether the
      plugin implements the already approved formula and must cite verbatim for-
      mula and code substrings. This check does not decide whether the approved
      formula itself is the correct empirical method.

Passing Step 4 is therefore not a claim that a factor has been correctly replicated. It
establishes a narrower proposition: the frozen signal code is syntactically valid, com-
patible with the approved interface, and has passed the stated leakage and execution
checks before deterministic backtesting.

F    REPRODUCIBILITY

The reported experiments use a local WRDS snapshot obtained in late June 2026. CRSP
monthly returns provide the returns backbone; Compustat annual fundamentals and
the relevant point-in-time link tables provide factor inputs where required. The data-
source registry records the source, columns, and linking rule used for each approved
MethodSpec. The present study records the month of data acquisition rather than
a vendor query identifier; readers should therefore treat exact reconstruction from a
fresh WRDS pull as a separate data-vintage exercise.

Each run carries a data-snapshot hash, a MethodSpec hash, a signal-code hash, a resolved-
configuration hash, and runtime provenance. For the final AssetGrowth batch, the
data-snapshot SHA–256 hash is e59e6493f8ac05f3af7d090a041787ccf77045eff130b3896022e2c6e32
the run artifacts record lifecycle commit b4fb8b0cbf48d7c4b2d0897fb9f46d24aea6df55-dirty
and frozen signal code hash 17d8f37c49504d47. Equivalent metadata are stored beside
every MeanRankRevGrowth and ShareVol run under runs/evidence/.

The semantic hashes identify the approved MethodSpec and resolved configuration
content; artifact SHA–256 hashes identify the materialized return-series and signal-
series files. A successful replication should match both layers: semantic agreement
establishes that the same specification and configuration were used, while artifact
agreement establishes that the same stored inputs and outputs were read. The ver-
sioned repository, resolved MethodSpecs, generated scripts, per-track metadata, and
comparison.json bundles together provide the audit trail for the numbers reported in
this thesis.
