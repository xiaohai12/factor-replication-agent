\begin{center}
  \LARGE \textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\vspace{1cm}

Factor replications can diverge because papers leave implementation choices
implicit. Universe, lag, breakpoints, weighting, and data treatment can change
the estimated premium and its significance. To address this problem, this
thesis uses a controlled workflow: an LLM produces an evidence-backed,
structured implementation specification (MethodSpec); human review resolves
ambiguity; and the approved signal is fixed. The analysis examines asset
growth, sales-growth reversal, and share turnover. The
asset-growth premium is replicated on the paper-aligned raw-return metric, but
a $+0.61\%$ configuration effect explains most of its $+0.68\%$ gap relative
to Chen and Zimmermann (C\&Z). Sales-growth reversal is replicated under the C\&Z
configuration but not under Hou, Xue, and Zhang's (HXZ) standardized settings.
For share turnover, a Fama--MacBeth coefficient and portfolio-sort spreads are
not comparable. The workflow distinguishes controlled configuration effects,
external differences, and estimand mismatch, so disagreement can be diagnosed
rather than reduced to a binary replication verdict.
