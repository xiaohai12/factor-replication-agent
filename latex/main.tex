\begin{center}
  \LARGE \textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\vspace{1cm}

Factor replication is often treated as binary, although discrepancies can
reflect paper interpretation, data environment, portfolio construction, or
estimand. This
thesis develops a controlled LLM-agent workflow for making those alternatives
auditable. The agent proposes an evidence-backed MethodSpec from a
paper, subject to human review, and generates only the signal-computation code
after approval.
All consequential empirical settings are selected from a fixed, reviewable
menu. The resulting signal is frozen before any configuration experiment,
so all empirical results are deterministic conditional on the approved
specification and data snapshot.

The workflow is applied to three illustrative equity anomalies. We compare
the papers' reported endpoints with C\&Z's independent human replication and
run the frozen agent signal under standardized HXZ configurations.
AssetGrowth is reproduced on the aligned raw-return metric, but its magnitude
is materially sensitive to configuration choices. MeanRankRevGrowth exhibits
a residual-dominated paper comparison, showing that a gap cannot automatically
be assigned to the agent when important conventions are unresolved or outside
the menu.
For ShareVol, the paper reports a Fama--MacBeth coefficient whereas the
available replications are portfolio-sort spreads; the system therefore
declines a paper-anchored numerical decomposition rather than comparing
incommensurable estimands.

Where factorial designs are feasible, Shapley values provide exact
within-engine attribution of configuration effects; larger designs receive OAT
sensitivity analysis with its interaction limitation explicit. External
comparisons and three-term identities remain observational accounting devices.
The contribution is thus not an autonomous empirical judge, but an
agent-assisted, human-reviewed process that turns paper interpretations into
versioned research objects and makes remaining replication gaps traceable
rather than opaque.
