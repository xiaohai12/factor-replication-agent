\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results are organized around three distinct empirical cases: one factor
for which the external teams agree, one whose paper-anchored comparison is
available but whose endpoints diverge, and one whose original estimand is not
comparable to the portfolio-sort output of this engine. Throughout, a reported
gap is not itself treated as evidence of a configuration effect: only a
complete factorial grid supports controlled Shapley attribution, while OAT
evidence and external comparisons retain their lower identification levels.

\subsection{RQ2: agent autonomy and capability boundary (cross-factor)}

All three factors reached executable frozen-signal tracks, so the pipeline
produced auditable outputs for the reviewed MethodSpecs. This is a completion
result, not an extraction-accuracy claim. The resolved MethodSpecs retain
their evidence status field by field: the resulting audit flags one weak field
for AssetGrowth, four for MeanRankRevGrowth, and thirteen for ShareVol. These
flags identify where human confirmation remains important; they do not
silently alter a backtest result. Because this study does not repeat
extraction from the same paper, it does not estimate within-agent sampling
variation, and the audit counts should not be interpreted as a probability
that the extracted method is correct.

The flags have two distinct meanings. An \emph{inferred} field is one for
which the paper gives an economic concept or a data source but not the native
database field or operational rule required by the engine. AssetGrowth's lone
flag is an inferred signal-input source column. MeanRankRevGrowth has an
inferred portfolio-sort mode and three inferred signal-input source columns.
ShareVol has inferred holding-period and signal-source details, alongside
three \emph{unspecified} portfolio-sort fields: the breakpoint basis, group
type, and sorting mode. ``Unspecified'' means that the paper itself does not
state the required implementation choice. The distinction matters: inference
is a documented mapping decision that can be checked against the source,
whereas an unspecified field requires a reviewer to supply a convention or
accept the engine default.

Figure~\ref{fig:assetgrowth-audit-trace} provides a representative audit
trace rather than reproducing every artifact for every factor. It links the
paper-evidence-backed MethodSpec from Steps~1--2, the frozen
\texttt{compute\_signal} formula generated in Step~3, the Step~4 validation
record, and the Step~5 executable-track metrics. The complete MethodSpecs,
source quotations, generated scripts, and realized signal series remain
versioned run artifacts; showing them in full in the results chapter would
obscure the empirical comparisons rather than make them more auditable.

\subsection{Per-factor results}

\subsubsection{AssetGrowth: agreement in external verdicts, but a material
configuration effect}

AssetGrowth \citep{CooperGulenSchill2008} is the sample's agreement case:
both C\&Z and HXZ report a positive, statistically significant result. In
the engine, the paper-faithful baseline has an in-sample $t$-statistic of
$4.12$; the C\&Z-configuration endpoint is $7.11$, while the standardized
HXZ endpoint is $3.60$. Table~\ref{tab:assetgrowth-endpoints} shows the
external references beside the three frozen-signal tracks. Relative to the
baseline, the HXZ-style configuration leaves the in-sample long--short return
positive but reduces it from $0.93\%$ to $0.71\%$/month and lowers the
$t$-statistic from $4.12$ to $3.60$; the C\&Z configuration instead raises
the return to $1.54\%$/month and the $t$-statistic to $7.11$. The C\&Z
paper-anchored identity attributes its
$1.03\%$/month total gap principally to the configuration term
($0.91\%$/month), with a $0.31\%$/month signal-and-environment residual and
a $-0.19\%$/month agent residual. These quantities are an accounting
identity across heterogeneous external endpoints; only the configuration term
is a controlled within-engine contrast.

Both configuration comparisons admit complete factorial grids. For the
C\&Z endpoint, the controlled Shapley decomposition assigns
$0.71\%$/month of the $0.61\%$/month within-engine difference to weighting,
offset by $-0.10\%$/month from the universe switch. For the standardized
endpoint, the breakpoint switch accounts for $-0.17\%$/month of the
$-0.22\%$/month difference, with the universe accounting for the remainder.
Thus, even in this agreement case, the configuration effect is economically
material and factor-specific rather than a generic implementation constant.

\subsubsection{MeanRankRevGrowth: a residual-dominated paper comparison}

For MeanRankRevGrowth \citep{LakonishokShleiferVishny1994}, the baseline,
C\&Z-configuration, and standardized-HXZ tracks have in-sample $t$-statistics
of $3.00$, $2.83$, and $1.33$, respectively. The paper-anchored identity is
available for both external references, but its largest term is the agent
replication residual: $-1.72\%$/month, compared with total gaps of
$-1.65\%$/month to C\&Z and $-2.39\%$/month to HXZ. This result should not
be read as a pure language-model error. The resolved baseline applies
documented defaults for accounting lag and missing returns where the reviewed
specification leaves fields unresolved or the menu cannot express them, so the
residual combines extraction, implementation, and finite-menu scope.

The two endpoint comparisons nevertheless have complete factorial grids.
For the standardized-HXZ endpoint, breakpoint, universe, and weighting each
make negative Shapley contributions, together accounting exactly for the
$-0.23\%$/month configuration difference. For the C\&Z endpoint, the
quantile switch contributes $-0.12\%$/month and the universe switch offsets
it by $0.02\%$/month. These are controlled attributions of the engine's own
configuration contrast; they do not identify the external teams' signal or
data-vintage differences.

\subsubsection{ShareVol: an intentionally non-comparable estimand}

ShareVol \citep{DatarNaikRadcliffe1998} is the boundary case. The paper's
headline result is a Fama--MacBeth coefficient, whereas the engine and the
external portfolio-sort references report long--short spreads. The system
therefore correctly marks both paper-anchored three-term identities as
unidentified rather than subtracting incomparable quantities. This is a
substantive result about the scope of the experiment, not a missing number to
be filled by approximation.

Its endpoint differences involve four and five fields, respectively, so the
controller uses OAT runs rather than a complete factorial grid. Accordingly,
no controlled Shapley attribution is available. The four standardized-side
OAT runs yield a $t$-statistic range of $0.79$ and one sign flip; this is
harmonized evidence of sensitivity, not an additive decomposition of the
endpoint gap. The baseline also has an off-menu weighting instruction and
other documented defaults, which further limits any paper-faithful reading.

\subsection{What the cross-factor comparison establishes}

The three cases do not support a single universal cause of replication
disagreement. AssetGrowth's C\&Z-side configuration difference is dominated
by weighting; MeanRankRevGrowth's controlled configuration effects are small
relative to its paper-comparison residual; and ShareVol cannot support the
same decomposition because its paper estimand differs and its field count
exceeds the factorial budget. The common finding is methodological: the
pipeline distinguishes controlled configuration evidence from external or
underidentified residuals instead of assigning every observed difference to a
single label.

\subsection{$t$-channel and post-publication evidence}

For AssetGrowth and MeanRankRevGrowth, eligible positive-return comparisons
permit the $t$-channel decomposition of Section~\ref{sec:design}. In the
AssetGrowth C\&Z endpoint comparison, the mean-return channel is the dominant
positive component of the log-$t$ change; its volatility component is also
positive and its sample-size component is near zero. ShareVol's mean returns
do not satisfy the positive-return requirement, so its $t$-channel outputs
are correctly marked degenerate and report only absolute-$t$ changes.

The post-publication split provides a separate descriptive check. AssetGrowth's
baseline moves from in-sample $t=4.12$ to post-publication $t=0.16$ and loses
significance. MeanRankRevGrowth's baseline remains significant
($t=3.00$ in sample and $t=2.53$ post-publication). ShareVol is insignificant
in both periods. These comparisons describe realized track histories; they do
not establish a causal publication effect.

\subsection{Evidence-constrained LLM diagnosis}

Step~8 was run as an optional explanation layer for all three completed
comparison bundles. It produced 25 persisted diagnosis claims (nine for
AssetGrowth, nine for MeanRankRevGrowth, and seven for ShareVol), with no
claim rejected by the evidence validator. Each accepted claim names one or
more keys from the deterministic bundle and inherits its identification level
and evidence strength. For example, the AssetGrowth proposal identifies the
weighting switch as the controlled Shapley attribution on the C\&Z line,
separately records its paired-switch and joint-test evidence, and labels
post-publication decay as observational. For ShareVol, the proposal reports
the unavailable paper-versus-track comparison as an evidence limitation rather
than inventing a comparable spread.

This demonstrates a bounded analytical capability: the model can select and
organize supported diagnostic claims across a heterogeneous evidence bundle.
It is not an independent empirical result or a validation of the model's
economic reasoning. Numeric statements in the rendered diagnosis are
reinserted deterministically from the bundle, and the artifacts retain the
status \texttt{llm\_assisted\_proposal}. The appropriate comparison is
therefore not ``AI versus deterministic results''--the latter are the source
of truth--but whether the proposal surfaces the same evidence, limitations,
and identification level as a human analyst. A blinded expert evaluation of
that question remains future work.
