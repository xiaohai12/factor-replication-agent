\section{Data and Factor Sample}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Data sources}

Portfolio returns are constructed from the CRSP Monthly Stock File,
including delisting returns; accounting inputs come
from Compustat Fundamentals Annual, linked to CRSP through the CCM link
table with a point-in-time accounting lag
(\texttt{accounting\_lag\_months}). IBES's unadjusted summary
statistics (annual EPS forecast consensus) are likewise registered, linked
to CRSP via a dedicated IBES--CRSP link table, though none of the three
factors in \S\ref{sec:selection} draws on it. Every source is declared once
in a single catalog (\S\ref{sec:pipeline}) rather than accreted ad hoc: each
entry states how that source's own native key links, through a
point-in-time link table, to the CRSP \texttt{permno} backbone every source
is ultimately merged onto. Adding a further source is a single declarative
entry in this catalog, after which any paper drawing on it is handled the
same way as CRSP/Compustat, rather than requiring new source-specific code;
conversely, a paper whose required source has no such entry is hard-blocked
at Step~2's review rather than silently improvised. This is also what makes
every field, join key, and lag behind a number in \S\ref{sec:results}
traceable to one place.

Chen and Zimmermann's factor metadata (\texttt{SignalDoc.csv}) plays a
strictly circumscribed role: it supplies $\CZ$ (each factor's reported
spread and $t$-statistic under C\&Z's own implementation) and the evidence
used to select factors in \S\ref{sec:selection}, and nothing else -- it is
never passed to the Step 1 extractor that produces $\Cagent$
(\S\ref{sec:framework}). The HXZ-style standardized configuration $\Cstd$
used to construct $\Ahxz$ is a single, versioned file
(\texttt{data/hxz/hxz\_standard\_config.yaml}), applied identically across
every factor rather than tuned per factor.

\subsection{Scope and assumptions}
\label{sec:scope}

This paper's implementation menu (Appendix~\ref{app:config-menu}) is
restricted along several dimensions that are worth stating positively
rather than apologetically: restricting attention to US common equity,
monthly portfolio returns, and CRSP--Compustat accounting fields, with
portfolio construction limited to univariate and independent-or-sequential
bivariate sorts estimated by portfolio sorting rather than cross-sectional
regression, is precisely what makes $\ImplSpace{P}$
(\S\ref{sec:framework}) something this paper can enumerate at all. A menu
with no boundary would make every disagreement unattributable, since there
would be no fixed list of candidate causes to attribute it to. The menu is a
deliberate boundary at any given time, not a permanent ceiling on what the
agent could support: unlike the data-source catalog of this section, where
adding a source is a single declarative entry, extending the menu means
implementing the corresponding logic in the engine itself, but nothing in
the design prevents doing so, and the menu has in fact grown as new factors
required it.

That said, the finite menu introduces a two-sided bias that must be stated
without qualification. It \emph{understates} underdetermination, because a
reasonable implementation choice that falls outside the menu can never
enter the disagreement band -- the band in \S\ref{sec:framework} is a lower
bound, not an unbiased estimate, for exactly this reason. It simultaneously
risks \emph{overstating} underdetermination, because a single extraction
draw conflates genuine paper ambiguity with ordinary language-model sampling
noise; only a repeated-draw study (\S\ref{sec:discussion}, left to future
work) could separate the two. Both directions of this bias are revisited
where they bear on a specific result in \S\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Factor selection criteria}
\label{sec:selection}

Factors are selected against one hard constraint and one sampling goal.
The constraint follows mechanically from \S\ref{sec:scope}: the signal,
universe, and estimator must all be computable from CRSP monthly data and
Compustat annual fields alone, using a single plain portfolio sort -- no
multi-way or within-group-conditioned sorts, no derived universe filters,
and no other estimator such as Fama--MacBeth. ShareVol is the sole
exception: its own headline test is a Fama-MacBeth coefficient, but
Hou-Xue-Zhang's and Chen-Zimmermann's own portfolio-sort reconstructions of
it satisfy the constraint, so it enters the sample on that basis.

Within that feasible set, the sampling goal is deliberately illustrative,
not representative: two factors are chosen for verified divergence between
Hou-Xue-Zhang's and Chen-Zimmermann's own reported verdicts, and one for
their agreement. The agreement case (asset growth, below) is the sample's
anchor -- a baseline where this pipeline's own gap decomposition should
turn up a small, well-explained gap, so the two disagreement cases read as
a genuine contrast rather than an artifact of a pipeline that only ever
finds gaps.

\subsection{The factor sample}

The final sample is three factors, chosen so that Hou-Xue-Zhang's and
Chen-Zimmermann's own reported verdicts on them differ in kind rather than
merely in degree (\S\ref{sec:intro}).

Asset growth \citep{CooperGulenSchill2008} is the sample's anchor: C\&Z's
own equal-weighted long-short spread is $1.73\%$/month ($t=8.45$), and
HXZ's own I/A-sorted, NYSE-VW spread is $t\approx 2.89$ -- both sides
significant and same-signed, the sample's sole instance of agreement.

Sales-growth reversal \citep{LakonishokShleiferVishny1994}
(\texttt{MeanRankRevGrowth}) is an opposite-verdict case compounded by a
menu-clamped delisting-return policy: C\&Z's own reported long-short return
is $0.55\%$/month ($t=3.94$). HXZ's own reported spread, by contrast, is a
clean $-0.19\%$/month
($t=1.08$) -- sign-reversed and statistically insignificant.

Share turnover \citep{DatarNaikRadcliffe1998} is an opposite-verdict case
whose original test is not a portfolio sort at all, so both teams'
comparison numbers are themselves reconstructions of the paper's method:
C\&Z's own long-short spread is significant ($t=8.86$), while HXZ's own is
$-0.11\%$/month ($t=0.46$) -- again sign-reversed and insignificant
(\S\ref{sec:selection}, \S\ref{sec:discussion}; Table~\ref{tab:roster}).
