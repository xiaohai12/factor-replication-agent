\section{Data and Factor Sample}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Data sources}

Portfolio returns are constructed from the CRSP Monthly Stock File (the
WRDS ``CIZ'' export), including delisting returns; accounting inputs come
from Compustat Fundamentals Annual, linked to CRSP through the CCM link
table with a point-in-time accounting lag
(\texttt{accounting\_lag\_months}). Every signal source and link table used
is registered in a single data-source registry (\S\ref{sec:pipeline}), so
the exact fields, join keys, and lags behind any number in
\S\ref{sec:results} are traceable to one place rather than to ad hoc code
scattered across factor-specific scripts.

Chen and Zimmermann's factor metadata (\texttt{SignalDoc.csv}) plays a
strictly circumscribed role: it supplies $\CZ$ (each factor's reported
spread and $t$-statistic under C\&Z's own implementation) and the evidence
used to select factors in \S\ref{sec:selection}, and nothing else -- it is
never passed to the Step 1 extractor that produces $\Cagent$
(\S\ref{sec:framework}). The HXZ-style standardized configuration $\Cstd$
used to construct $\Ahxz$ is a single, versioned file
(\texttt{data/hxz/hxz\_standard\_config.yaml}), applied identically across
every factor rather than tuned per factor.

\subsection{Scope and assumptions}
\label{sec:scope}

This paper's implementation menu (Appendix~\ref{app:config-menu}) is
restricted along several dimensions that are worth stating positively
rather than apologetically: restricting attention to US common equity,
monthly portfolio returns, and CRSP--Compustat accounting fields, with
portfolio construction limited to univariate and independent bivariate
sorts estimated by portfolio sorting rather than cross-sectional
regression, is precisely what makes $\ImplSpace{P}$
(\S\ref{sec:framework}) something this paper can enumerate at all. A menu
with no boundary would make every disagreement unattributable, since there
would be no fixed list of candidate causes to attribute it to.
\input{tables/tab_scope_assumptions}

That said, the finite menu introduces a two-sided bias that must be stated
without qualification. It \emph{understates} underdetermination, because a
reasonable implementation choice that falls outside the menu can never
enter the disagreement band -- the band in \S\ref{sec:framework} is a lower
bound, not an unbiased estimate, for exactly this reason. It simultaneously
risks \emph{overstating} underdetermination, because a single extraction
draw conflates genuine paper ambiguity with ordinary language-model sampling
noise; only a repeated-draw study (\S\ref{sec:discussion}, left to future
work) could separate the two. Both directions of this bias are revisited
where they bear on a specific result in \S\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Factor selection criteria}
\label{sec:selection}

Three constraints follow mechanically from \S\ref{sec:scope} and are
non-negotiable: a factor's headline result must be a portfolio-sort
long-short spread and $t$-statistic, not a Fama--MacBeth coefficient, since
the engine implements only the former; its signal must be computable from
CRSP monthly data and Compustat annual fields alone; and its universe
filters must not require a derived column (e.g., years since a firm's first
Compustat observation) or an asymmetric, non-equal-quantile split, neither
of which this engine's universe-filter machinery supports.

Within that feasible set, factors were then selected for the specific,
verified divergence evidence they carry between Hou-Xue-Zhang and Chen and
Zimmermann -- not merely for being underdetermined by their own paper in
isolation. Candidates were drawn from a systematic cross-check against both
teams' own reported verdicts (rather than inferred solely from C\&Z's
\texttt{Notes} field), covering cases where the two teams agree, disagree in
either direction, or disagree for a reason attributable to data vintage
rather than methodology.
